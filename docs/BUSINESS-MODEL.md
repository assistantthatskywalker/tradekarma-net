# TradeKarma business model and validation plan

Canonical commercial plan — 7 September 2026. Supersedes the economics and token roles in the February first-draft business plan. Figures below are scenarios, not forecasts or achieved results.

## Buyer and value

Start with a small cohort of merchants accessible through the founder's existing network. Select one product category only after confirming merchant demand, payment-provider support, and market-specific legal requirements. CBD experience may aid access; it is not proof of regulatory simplicity or product-market fit.

Sell independently measurable review usefulness, dependable fulfillment, repeat purchases, and reduced fraud/support expense. Do not reward favorable sentiment. Paid placement must be labeled and separate from reputation rankings.

A pilot should integrate an existing merchant order source before building a broad two-sided marketplace. Use fulfilled, non-refunded order evidence, a verified account binding, and event uniqueness. Compare eligible customers assigned to reward and control cohorts; pre-register outcome definitions and include fraud and reward cost.

## Revenue tests

Test merchant subscriptions and transaction-service fees. The historical CHF 29–99/month and 3–5% fee ranges are hypotheses, not established willingness to pay. Do not stack a processing spread unless payment permissions, costs and customer value support it. Offer neither guaranteed returns nor token purchase incentives during the pilot.

## Unit economics

For each merchant cohort record:

- Eligible fulfilled orders, average order value, net GMV after refunds.
- Net subscription revenue and net transaction revenue, excluding collected taxes.
- Payment costs, fraud/chargebacks, variable support cost, reward cost and gas.
- Contribution margin = net revenue minus all attributable variable costs.
- Retention, repeat purchase rate, externally scored review usefulness, dispute rate.
- Acquisition spend per retained paying merchant; payback based on contribution, not GMV.
- Cash runway and separate operating/reward reserves.

For any proposed reward distribution, use max(0, cash available after operating needs, liabilities, tax provisions and reserve targets), subject to legal/accounting review. Never distribute funds already reserved for redemption. The old annual revenue and cost range midpoints imply a CHF 47,700 deficit, not a funded launch reward pool.

## Token necessity and governance

KRUNE: non-transferable reputation history. Define correction/revocation and wallet recovery before production; spending belongs in a distinct loyalty-credit system. Existing staking assumes non-decreasing KRUNE.

KDEX: optional later mechanism. Before launch publish rights, exact supply, distribution addresses, independently enforced team vesting, voting snapshot rules, treasury powers, and concentration analysis. A fixed supply does not cause demand. Prototype voting snapshots are not a deployed DAO.

KSHRD: compare direct funded claims against a transferable claim token. Document incremental user benefit and incremental custody, liquidity, regulatory, security and accounting costs. Default to no launch without evidence that the extra instrument is worthwhile.

Keepback bonus: any 10% KDEX bonus is an explicit marketing subsidy with a reserve budget and measured incremental retention. Keep the contract branch disabled until pricing and user execution protection are implemented.

## Economic stress tests

Simulate zero/negative operating cash flow, uneven fee timing, the empty-pool backlog, concentrated capital with minimal reputation, account splitting, paid reviews, issuer compromise, KDEX price collapse, and USDC unavailability. Report top-holder pool share and net benefit to each attacker after realistic acquisition/fraud costs. The simple geometric mean is not a Sybil proof.

## Decisions before launch

The owner and merchant pilot must determine pricing, target market, evidence-verification policy, dispute handling, reward eligibility/aging, reserves, token necessity, vesting, and recovery. Legal counsel and an independent auditor must assess the resulting concrete design. Repository tests cannot complete those external gates.
